## 1. Verification findings

1. Start with distinctive wording, trace citations and identify a specific work/edition/location. A thematic quotation list or attractive attribution is a lead, not proof. Library guidance and the retained Quote Investigator case illustrate that a plausible attribution can remain unsupported.
2. Distinguish earliest documented occurrence, authorship, a speaker quoting someone else, published translation, later edition and modern compilation. A literal match in a book is necessary but may not establish the book author as originator. The retained Montessori examples separate the author's prose from a nearby attributed Dante line.
3. Preserve exact wording, capitalization and scope. A clause is not a complete sentence; adding an initial capital, rewriting historic pronouns or inserting a period can fabricate a variant. Choose another quotation rather than silently modernizing it under the same attribution.
4. Keep contextual and rights checks separate from substring checks. Autobiographical testimony, philosophical claims and poetry are not evidence of learning efficacy, grading rules or monetary policy.
5. Public-domain status is territorial and edition/translation dependent. Gutenberg's US determination is not India/worldwide commercial clearance. Short motivational display use is not automatically licensed by a historical date, an educational theme or a research citation.
