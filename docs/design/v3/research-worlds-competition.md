### Metaphor versus information

Student Multiverse and Mentor Dimensions are artistic directions. None of the opened sources proves a physical multiverse or uniquely correct palette, geometry, lore or badge system. The original brief's assignments remain unchanged; no new world vocabulary, currency/league name, shape family, asset, typography or sound setting is finalized in B.

A higher-dimensional projection is a representation with limits. G1's coordinate-dropping projection can collapse different four-dimensional points into the same three-dimensional point. G2's stereographic map is one-to-one on the sphere except the projection point, but distorts scale: points near that point appear far away and can extend toward infinity. These are different mechanisms. Apparent enlargement or inside-out motion in a projection is not physical size change and may be disorienting if used without purpose. Neither source tests a learner-facing interface.

Original art should not be equated with cryptic status. W2-06 supports one meaning per symbol and proximity to the relevant content; W2-05 prevents rank or eligibility from depending on hue alone. This informs later comparison of designs, not a C-stage component specification. Dense badge clutter, tiny secondary text and constant motion would still need evaluation against the A2 readability/accessibility findings.

## 3. Competition/mission findings: B5

### Activity, motivation and rank are not interchangeable

W5-01's retrieved university abstract describes a2×4online image-annotation experiment. Points, levels and leaderboards increased generated-tag quantity, but competence/intrinsic motivation did not significantly differ from control. Full methods and sample size were not opened; no effect size or educational-retention inference is claimed. More visible activity is not automatically better learning or intrinsic motivation.

W5-02 investigated manipulated leaderboard feedback in427participants, students from two German universities. Its methods specify four position/trend conditions plus a no-feedback control. Leaderboards were static fictitious rankings, unrelated to actual performance. The abstract's wording suggests five leaderboard conditions plus control, but the methods describe four plus control; this ambiguity is retained rather than silently repeated.

Higher/upward feedback had the highest reported interest/enjoyment and lower/downward the lowest; performance differences were small to negligible in the reported task. This is evidence for taking comparison feedback seriously, not a deployed-course retention result, a universal harm claim or a license to fabricate favorable rankings. Participant exclusions, task context and university population constrain transfer to Orbit or Indian learners. The paper's public release was2025-12-09 and journal issue2026; PDF repository creation metadata is not its publication date.

### Semantics and user control

W5-03 recommends native table semantics where applicable to static tabular facts. A table is not automatically an interactive grid; managed keyboard navigation is a separate interaction model. A decorative world can surround comparative facts without replacing the semantic access to rank, score and status. This is not a final leaderboard layout.

W5-04 discusses user control and resumable tasks in complex services, but warns against a task list when a fixed order is required. Learning prerequisites must therefore not be presented as freely reorderable missions merely for visual polish. Government service guidance is an analogy, not evidence that a mission system improves mastery.

Personal best, nearby cohorts, voluntary comparison, constructive next actions and visible progress remain design hypotheses for C. The opened studies do not prove those specific remedies, score calibration, privacy policy or premium styling. No cohort/money/reward/rank policy is finalized by these findings. The two shared W3C documents directly support non-color tier/status meaning and interpretable mission icons.
