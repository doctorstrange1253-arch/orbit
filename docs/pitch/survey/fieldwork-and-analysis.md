## Pre-registered cleaning/reporting

1. Freeze eligibility, survey/version/window, exclusion rules, denominators, segments and claims before seeing results. Record later deviations; do not selectively remove inconvenient answers.
2. Separate funnel counts actually observable: eligible/consenting/submitted/retained/valid per question. A submission is not a signup, unique person, active user or purchase.
3. Remove technical duplicate exports by anonymous submission ID under a declared first-complete rule. If IDs unavailable, unique-person deduplication is unverified. Identical opinions alone are not duplicates; no device/IP fingerprinting to enforce uniqueness.
4. Counts/hours: flag negative/noninteger values,7dayhours>168, overlapping mentor totals>168, finished/stopped>started or their sum>started. Reject the affected measure, not all other usable answers, unless consent/eligibility invalid.
5. Distinguish routed-out/skipped/not applicable/cannot judge/prefer not to answer/missing. None is not an omitted response. Do not recode missingness as zero, neutral or favorable intent. Report each numerator, valid denominator and missing count.
6. Describe actual n, broad population, dates, channels and convenience sampling. Response rate requires a known eligible, deduplicated invitation frame; group membership/public-link views are not that denominator.
7. No probability-sampling margin of error or nationally representative confidence interval for this convenience sample. Use descriptive counts; no causal learning gains, national prevalence, conversion or market-share claims.
8. Predeclare exploratory cuts such as year/city type/device; show segment n and suppress identifying small cells, particularly<5. Avoid substantive comparisons where a group has<10valid answers. These are provisional reporting safeguards, not statistical power proof.
9. Criticism and contrary evidence stay visible. Quotes require consent/redaction; faithfully quote, or label paraphrases. Do not invent quotes. Commitment ledger is separate from anonymous opinion scores.

## Van Westendorp: declared calculation convention

Price module cannot run without one consistent hypothetical monthly offer card; no existing retail target as anchor. T=too cheap/quality doubt, C=cheap/good value, E=expensive but consider, X=too expensive/not consider; whole INR/month.
Complete-price subset: four numeric nonnegative integers with T≤C≤E≤X. Genuine zero allowed; cannot judge is missing. No silent swaps/rounding. Extreme values get sensitivity checks; any form cap declared before collection. Report missing/nonmonotonic/wrong-unit/excluded counts and valid n.
At candidate p, TC(p)=share(T≥p), Cheap(p)=share(C≥p), Expensive(p)=share(E≤p), TE(p)=share(X≤p). The first two decrease; the latter increase. Ties/inclusive comparisons are part of this declared convention.
OPP: TC∩TE; IPP: Cheap∩Expensive. This plan uses six-curve boundaries: NotCheap=1−Cheap, NotExpensive=1−Expensive; PMC=TC∩NotCheap and PME=TE∩NotExpensive. Alternative four-curve boundary conventions exist; never mix labels/conventions. This is an analysis proposal needing expert/method review, not price-setting authority.
Evaluate on sorted observed-price grid plus zero, with explicitly declared interpolation; ties/plateaus yield intervals. If no identifiable crossing, report not estimable rather than manufacture a preferred price. Check curve directions/range0–1, units and thresholds first.
“Optimal” in OPP is a method-defined perception point, not profit-maximizing price, observed willingness to purchase, affordability or revenue. No intersections have been computed from real data.
