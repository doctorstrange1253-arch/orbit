# Speaker notes — v4

Five-minute main pitch; backup slides are Q&A only. All quantitative scenarios are estimates, not real bills/traction.

## 01 — Cover (20 sec)

Orbit starts with a question: what if the next course began with a skill students actually requested? We are developing a learner-led platform connecting demand with mentors and a visible learning journey. It is a prototype. We will separate source-visible work, proposed milestones and what remains unproven.

Sources: PRODUCT

## 02 — 01 Problem (25 sec)

Our first problem hypothesis concerns adult college learners seeking a practical skill. Finding relevant instruction and continuing without feedback can be difficult. The founder and friends experienced this, but we have no validated prevalence figure. We still need a specific dated example. Our first test should address one skill and a clearly defined learner group, not everyone.

Sources: FOUNDER

## 03 — 02 Customer insight (25 sec)

Customer evidence is our largest weakness. No one-to-one interviews or primary survey dataset has been supplied. We have prepared an adult student micro-survey and mentor conversations. Targets are not results: only actual responses will enter the deck with n, population, date and method. Real course requests and written pilot commitments matter more than favorable opinions.

Sources: FOUNDER

## 04 — 03 Solution (30 sec)

The proposed loop is request, validate demand, mentor commitment and course creation. Students name an unmet topic, others support it, and a mentor decides whether that demand justifies building. We are not claiming the first such idea or dismissing existing catalogs. We want to test relevance and a closer mentor relationship. Advanced confidence-ranked notification waves are still next.

Sources: PRODUCT

## 05 — 04 How it works (25 sec)

The intended path connects a request and mentor response to finite video lessons, a visible journey, quizzes and feedback. This image is a real historical prototype capture using test data. It is not a current paid course or proof of learning improvement. Before pitch day, the team must identify and rehearse only flows it can personally repeat.

Sources: PRODUCT

## 06 — 05 Progress (30 sec)

Source-visible prototype work includes requests and votes, course journey interfaces and mentor recording and review controls. The integrated pilot remains unproven. Recurring checkout, actual bank settlement and stronger AI review and mentor-wave workflows still need integration. These are material limitations, not hidden polish items. Our next proof is an integrated pilot measuring demand, usability and commercial readiness.

Sources: PRODUCT

## 07 — 06 Market (25 sec)

We propose starting with adult GEU students in selected years and branches who need one practical skill. The founder must confirm this beachhead and its relevant official population. Serviceable demand is population multiplied by reachable share and demonstrated need. One hundred paying students in twelve months is a target, not a forecast. Initial mentors need teachable proof and time to pilot.

Sources: FOUNDER

## 08 — 07 Business model (35 sec)

Subscriptions target twenty percent for the platform and an eighty-percent pool for mentors of the learner's enrolled courses. One-to-one sessions have a separate fifteen-percent platform fee. Core599 and Premium1199 are proposed targets, not live prices. Our illustrative model deducts gateway, video, AI and support costs. Actual bills, tax basis, usage and refund rules determine margin; scenarios are not profit promises.

Sources: PRODUCT, RZP, CF

## 09 — 08 Action plan (30 sec)

In the first month, proposed targets are five genuine mentor commitments, two reviewed courses and thirty activated learners. By day sixty, we aim to complete one request-to-launch cycle and test ten paid conversions only after checkout is ready. By day ninety, the target is a second cycle and twenty-five paying learners. Clubs, ambassadors and mentor outreach are channels to test; every milestone needs a named owner.

Sources: PRODUCT

## 10 — 09 Team (25 sec)

A team exists, but names, roles and contributions must be supplied. We will not invent credentials or assign all repository work to an unnamed founder. Engineering owns integration, the content lead owns mentors and course quality, and customer development owns discovery and campus activation. People can hold multiple roles; we should disclose capacity honestly. Photos and advisors appear only if genuine.

Sources: FOUNDER

## 11 — 10 The ask (30 sec)

We propose up to two lakh rupees, subject to the panel's decision. Infrastructure and first-course mentor work take priority, then activation, professional review, testing, equipment and contingency. These are allocations awaiting quotes. The first lakh supports a minimum pilot; the next fifty thousand adds a second cycle. We borrow equipment and cut paid campaigns first if funding is smaller. Beyond money, we need mentor introductions and pilot access.

Sources: FORM
